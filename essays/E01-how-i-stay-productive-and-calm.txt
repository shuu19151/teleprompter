HOW I STAY PRODUCTIVE AND CALM
AT WORK

When people ask me what my job
is like, I usually say, "It's busy,
but I enjoy it."

I work in an office in the
city, and my days are rarely
the same.

Some mornings I spend hours answering emails
and joining meetings.

Other days I work quietly on a
report, trying to finish it before
a deadline.

Over the years, I've learned that being
productive is not about working harder.

It's about working in a smarter and
calmer way.

Today I'd like to share a few
habits that have helped me a lot.

The first habit is planning my day
before it starts.

Every morning, before I open my inbox,
I take ten minutes to write down
the three most important things I need
to finish that day.

I used to write very long lists
with fifteen or twenty tasks, but I
always felt disappointed at the end of
the day because I could never finish
them all.

Now I focus on three things only.

If I finish them early, great, I
can take on something extra.

If not, at least I know I
completed the work that really mattered.

The second habit is managing my communication.

Email and messages can easily take over
your whole day.

At first, I checked my phone every
few minutes because I was afraid of
missing something important.

But I noticed that I was constantly
interrupted, and it was hard to concentrate.

So I decided to check my email
only at certain times: once in the
morning, once after lunch, and once before
I go home.

If something is really urgent, people can
call me or talk to me directly.

This small change gave me much more
time for deep work, and honestly, I
feel less stressed.

The third habit is taking short breaks.

In the past, I often skipped lunch
or ate at my desk while working.

I thought I was saving time, but
by the afternoon I was tired and
made silly mistakes.

Now I always step away from my
computer for at least twenty minutes
at lunchtime.

Sometimes I take a short walk around
the block, and sometimes I just have
a coffee and chat with a colleague.

Those small conversations are important, too.

They help me build good relationships with
my team, and they remind me that
work is not only about tasks and
deadlines, but also about people.

Another thing that makes a big difference
is asking for help early.

When I was new to the company,
I was nervous about asking questions.

I worried that my colleagues would think
I wasn't capable.

One day, I spent almost three hours
trying to fix a problem in
a spreadsheet.

Finally, I asked a coworker, and she
solved it in five minutes.

I felt a little embarrassed, but she
smiled and said, "Don't worry, I had
the same problem last year."

Since then, I've never hesitated to ask.

Most people are happy to help, and
it saves everyone a lot of time.

Of course, there are still difficult days.

Sometimes a client changes their mind at
the last minute, or a project doesn't
go as planned.

On those days, I try to stay
calm and remember that every problem has
a solution.

I take a deep breath, think about
what I can control, and start with
the smallest step.

Last month, for example, a client asked
us to completely change a presentation the
night before the meeting.

I felt panic at first, but I
called my team, we shared the work,
and we finished it together before midnight.

I also try to learn from
each situation.

After a tough week, I often ask
myself, "What went well, and what could
I do differently next time?"

This simple question helps me improve step
by step.

Finally, I make sure I have a
life outside of work.

After I leave the office, I try
not to check work emails.

I spend time with my family, go
to the gym, or watch a movie.

At first, it was difficult to switch
off, but now I understand that rest
is part of being productive.

When I'm relaxed and well rested, I
think more clearly and work more efficiently
the next day.

In conclusion, staying productive and calm at
work doesn't require anything complicated.

It just needs a few simple habits:
plan your day, control your communication, take
breaks, ask for help, and protect your
free time.

I'm still learning, and I'm sure I
will keep improving.

But these habits have already made my
work life much more enjoyable, and I
would encourage anyone to try them.

Thank you for listening.
